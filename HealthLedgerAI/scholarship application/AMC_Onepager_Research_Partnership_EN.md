## Reducing Billing Errors and Strengthening Process Control

**A proposed research partnership between American Medical Center and European University Cyprus**

---

**The problem**

In hospital revenue cycles a substantial share of avoidable cost sits in administrative processing: coding and billing errors, manual rework, claims rejected by insurers or the national health system, delayed reimbursement, and findings in internal and external audits. These costs arise in different departments and are rarely aggregated into a single figure — which is why they are rarely managed as one.

**The project**

Doctoral research at European University Cyprus, supervised by **Prof. Alexios Kythreotis**, Deputy Dean of the School of Business Administration and Digital Technologies. Three phases over three years:

1. **Baseline.** Map where errors arise in the accounting and billing workflow, how often, and what they cost in rework hours and delayed or lost revenue.
2. **Intervention.** Test AI-supported detection of error patterns before claims are submitted.
3. **Evaluation.** Quantify the effect on error rates, processing time and cost, with a cross-border comparison against a second hospital (European Interbalkan Medical Center, Thessaloniki).

**What the hospital gains**

- A quantified picture of its own administrative error costs — a diagnostic most hospitals never commission.
- Tested and documented recommendations rather than a theoretical report.
- A benchmark against a comparable institution in another health system.
- First access to all findings before publication.
- Association with peer-reviewed research and visibility as a clinical innovation partner.

No licence fee, no software purchase, no consulting invoice.

**What the hospital would provide**

Access to anonymised process and billing data, a contact person in finance or billing, and a limited number of staff interviews.

**Data protection**

All data anonymised or pseudonymised before analysis. Processing governed by a written agreement between the hospital and the university. No patient-identifying data leaves the hospital. Ethics approval obtained from European University Cyprus before fieldwork begins.

**A funding route that costs the hospital little: "PhD in Industry"**

The Cypriot **Research and Innovation Foundation** operates a programme under which a Cypriot enterprise hosts a doctoral researcher it employs, while that researcher is registered in a doctoral programme at a Cypriot university. Funding of **up to €150,000 over three years** supports the project.

- The **enterprise is the applicant**; the university participates as academic partner.
- Priority is given to enterprises that have not received funding from the Foundation or from Horizon Europe since 2016, and to medium and large enterprises.
- The hospital's contribution is largely administrative. The research itself is externally financed.

Exact eligibility and the date of the next call would be confirmed with the Foundation before any application is prepared. Should the programme not fit, two lighter alternatives exist: a research partnership combining data access with a modest contribution to costs, or part-time employment in the finance function with protected research time.
